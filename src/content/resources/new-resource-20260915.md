The line graph illustrates the average monthly percentage change in the prices of copper, nickel and zinc in 2014, compared with the previous month.

Overall, nickel experienced by far the greatest fluctuations during the year, while copper showed the most stable pattern. All three metals generally declined during the first half of the year before recovering towards the end, with their prices all recording positive monthly changes in November and December.

At the beginning of the year, nickel saw the most dramatic increase, rising by approximately 6% in January. However, this figure fell sharply to 4% in February and then to around 1% in March. By contrast, copper increased by about 2% in January before its monthly growth gradually declined to around 0.5% in March and April. Zinc followed a slightly different pattern, climbing from 1% in January to a peak of 3% in February, after which its rate of increase steadily fell.

The downward trend became more noticeable around the middle of the year. In June, nickel recorded its lowest figure, at approximately -3%, while zinc fell to about -1% and copper decreased slightly to around -0.5%. From July to September, copper recovered and remained stable at roughly 1%, whereas nickel stayed at around -1% and zinc at approximately -0.5%.

In October, nickel fell again to about -2%, while zinc also declined to around -1%. Nevertheless, all three metals rebounded considerably in the final two months. By December, zinc showed the strongest monthly increase, at approximately 2%, compared with about 1.5% for copper and 1% for nickel.
